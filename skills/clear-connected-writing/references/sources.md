# Public writing guidance

These links support selected principles in the skill. The operational choices and examples are an editorial synthesis, not a university-endorsed method. The guidance concerns prose and revision; it does not prescribe Harvard author-date referencing or any other citation system.

- [Harvard College Writing Center: Anatomy of a Body Paragraph](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/anatomy-body-paragraph). Supports developing a paragraph through a meaningful point, evidence and analysis. The skill treats these as functions rather than a compulsory sentence sequence.
- [Harvard College Writing Center: Transitions](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/transitions). Supports carrying familiar information into new information and expressing the actual relationship between ideas.
- [Harvard College Writing Center: Tips for Organizing Your Essay](https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/tips-organizing-your-essay). Supports reverse outlining to inspect paragraph order, gaps and repetition.
- [Purdue OWL: Sentence Variety](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/academic_writing/sentence_variety/index.html). Supports purposeful variation in sentence length, structure and openings, including short sentences for emphasis.
- [University of Melbourne: Writing with GenAI](https://students.unimelb.edu.au/academic-skills/study-skills/learning-with-genai/writing-with-genai). Offers approaches to feedback on expression and flow while keeping judgement with the writer. Its suggested feedback-only tasks do not mean that all authorised revision must be limited to feedback. Apply the rules of the relevant setting.

Consult current source guidance if making a claim about an institution's requirements. These general writing resources do not establish permission to use AI for a particular assessment or workplace task, and they do not support promises about evading AI detection.
